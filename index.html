📚 Bounded-Buffer & Semaphore Mechanics Guide
Theoretical Architecture
💡 The Core Problem

Two asynchronous processes share a fixed-size memory pool (buffer):

  • Producer generates data items and stores them into buffer.
  • Consumer extracts and processes items from buffer.
  • Constraint: Producer must sleep if buffer is FULL; Consumer must sleep if buffer is EMPTY.
🔑 Semaphores Explained
  • mutex = 1: Binary lock protecting in/out index pointers against concurrent data corruption.
  • empty = N: Counting semaphore tracking available vacant slots.
  • full = 0: Counting semaphore tracking ready data items.
📐 Key Mathematical Invariants

At every point in time during correct synchronization:

  • empty + full = BUFFER_SIZE (6)
  • 0 ≤ full ≤ 6 & 0 ≤ empty ≤ 6
  • in = (in + 1) % 6 (Wraps around circularly)
  • out = (out + 1) % 6 (Avoids buffer shifting)
🛡️ Race Conditions Avoided
  • Lost Updates: Two producers writing to slot in simultaneously is impossible because wait(mutex) serializes buffer access.
  • Buffer Underflow: Consumers cannot read from an empty slot because wait(full) blocks until full > 0.

💡 First & Last Reader Pattern
  • First Reader (read_count == 1): Must execute wait(rw_mutex) to lock the database, blocking all incoming writers.
  • Middle Readers (read_count > 1): Enter immediately without touching rw_mutex.
  • Last Reader (read_count == 0): Executes signal(rw_mutex), freeing database for waiting writers.
⚠️ Writer Starvation Hazard

Under First Readers-Writers (Reader Preference):

  • If new readers keep arriving while previous readers are still reading, read_count never drops to 0.
  • A waiting writer can remain starved indefinitely!
  • Solution: Fair / Writer-Preference protocols introduce an additional turnstile semaphore.
🛡️ Role of the mutex Semaphore

Why do we need mutex if we already have rw_mutex?

  • mutex provides mutual exclusion exclusively for modifying the shared variable read_count.
  • Without it, two readers executing read_count++ concurrently could cause lost increments (a classic race condition).

📚 Coffman's 4 Conditions & Deadlock Resolution
Dijkstra's Classic Model
🔒 Coffman's 4 Deadlock Conditions
  • 1. Mutual Exclusion: A chopstick can only be held by 1 philosopher at a time.
  • 2. Hold and Wait: A philosopher holds left chopstick while waiting for right chopstick.
  • 3. No Preemption: Chopsticks cannot be forcibly seized from a philosopher.
  • 4. Circular Wait: Each philosopher waits for a resource held by the next in the ring.
🛡️ Breaking Circular Wait (Asymmetric Solution)

Dijkstra proposed an asymmetric pickup order:

  • Even Philosophers (0, 2, 4): Pick up LEFT fork first, then RIGHT fork.
  • Odd Philosophers (1, 3): Pick up RIGHT fork first, then LEFT fork.
  • This breaks the circular chain, guaranteeing at least one philosopher can acquire both forks!
⚡ Maximum Concurrency Limit

How many philosophers can eat simultaneously?

  • Each eating philosopher requires 2 adjacent chopsticks.
  • In a system of 5 philosophers, at most ⌊5 / 2⌋ = 2 non-adjacent philosophers can eat at once (e.g., P0 & P2, or P1 & P3).
⚠️ Starvation vs Deadlock
  • Deadlock: Complete freeze where all 5 philosophers wait forever in a cycle.
  • Starvation: A philosopher might starve if their two neighbors conspire to alternate eating endlessly, though deadlock has not occurred.

📚 Sleeping Barber Queueing & Rendezvous Dynamics
Client-Server Pattern
💡 The Problem Analogy

A classic client-server queueing synchronization problem:

  • Barber sits in cutting chair; sleeps if no customers.
  • Customer arrives: wakes the barber if asleep.
  • If barber is busy and chairs available: sits and waits.
  • If barber busy and all chairs filled: customer balks (leaves).
🔑 Semaphores & Mutex Breakdown
  • customers: Counting semaphore initialized to 0. Number of customers waiting.
  • barber: Binary semaphore initialized to 0. Barber signals customer when ready to cut.
  • mutex: Binary lock protecting read/write access to shared integer waiting.
🤝 The Rendezvous Concept

Notice the two-way handshake:

  • Customer executes signal(customers) then wait(barber).
  • Barber executes wait(customers) then signal(barber).
  • Neither customer nor barber can proceed with the haircut without the other being ready!
🚪 Handling Capacity Balking

Real-world queue limits in Operating Systems:

  • Similar to packet drop in full router buffers or socket rejection on maximum pending connections (listen(backlog)).
  • Prevents infinite memory accumulation during sudden workload spikes.
